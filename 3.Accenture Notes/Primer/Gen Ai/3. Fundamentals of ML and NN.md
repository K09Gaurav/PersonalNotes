## Question 1

### Question
In the context of neural networks, what does the term "backpropagation" refer to?

- A. The process of adding more layers
- B. The initial random assignment of weights
- C. The activation of neurons in the hidden layer
- D. The method of adjusting weights based on the error
- E. The forward flow of data

---

### Answer
**The method of adjusting weights based on the error**

---

### Explanation

#### Why Correct Answer is Correct
Backpropagation is the learning algorithm used to train neural networks. After the network makes a prediction, the error is calculated using a loss function. This error is then propagated backward through the network to determine how each weight contributed to the error.

The weights are adjusted using gradients so that future predictions become more accurate.

Important exam points:
- Backpropagation occurs after forward propagation.
- It uses gradients and the chain rule from calculus.
- It helps minimize the loss function.

---

#### Why Option A is Wrong
Adding more layers changes the architecture but is unrelated to backpropagation.

---

#### Why Option B is Wrong
Random weight initialization occurs before training begins.

---

#### Why Option C is Wrong
Neuron activation occurs during forward propagation.

---

#### Why Option D is Correct
Backpropagation updates weights based on prediction errors.

---

#### Why Option E is Wrong
The forward flow of data is called forward propagation, not backpropagation.
