## Question 1

### Question
Which problem arises when training RNNs on long sequences?

- A. High bias
- B. Underfitting
- C. Overfitting
- D. Vanishing or exploding gradients
- E. All of the given options

---

### Answer
**Vanishing or exploding gradients**

---

### Explanation

#### Why Correct Answer is Correct
When RNNs process long sequences, gradients are repeatedly multiplied during backpropagation through time (BPTT).

This can lead to:

- **Vanishing gradients** → Gradients become extremely small, preventing learning of long-term dependencies.
- **Exploding gradients** → Gradients become extremely large, causing unstable training.

These are the most well-known challenges in traditional RNNs.

Important exam points:
- Long sequences → Gradient problems
- LSTM and GRU were developed to mitigate these issues

---

#### Why Option A is Wrong
High bias refers to underfitting and is not the characteristic long-sequence problem.

---

#### Why Option B is Wrong
Underfitting can occur but is not the classic RNN challenge.

---

#### Why Option C is Wrong
Overfitting is unrelated to sequence length.

---

#### Why Option D is Correct
This is the standard answer in RNN theory.

---

#### Why Option E is Wrong
The question asks for the specific long-sequence issue.

After training, what can be inferred if the validation loss keeps decreasing but training loss remains high?

- A. Model architecture is flawed
- B. Model is underfitting
- C. Model is overfitting
- D. Model is perfectly trained
- E. Training data is corrupted

---

### Answer
**Model is underfitting**

---

### Explanation

#### Why Correct Answer is Correct
If training loss remains relatively high, the model has not learned the training data effectively.

A persistently high training loss generally indicates insufficient learning capacity or inadequate training.

Important exam points:
- High training loss → Underfitting
- Low training loss + High validation loss → Overfitting

---

### Exam Tip

This question is somewhat unusual because, in practice, validation loss continuously decreasing while training loss remains high is not a common pattern.

Most certification-style quizzes expect:

- High training loss → Underfitting
- Low training loss + High validation loss → Overfitting

Therefore, **Underfitting** is the expected exam answer.

---

#### Why Option A is Wrong
The architecture may still be valid.

---

#### Why Option B is Correct
The model has not fit the training data sufficiently.

---

#### Why Option C is Wrong
Overfitting typically shows low training loss and worse validation performance.

---

#### Why Option D is Wrong
Perfect training would not have high training loss.

---

#### Why Option E is Wrong
Corrupted data cannot be inferred from the given information.
